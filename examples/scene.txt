# Each clause is the English, then an indented line of what it means: the verb, then role:noun.
# The verb be says what its subject is, with noun:fool or adjective:big in place of an object, or
# where it is, with location:house.
# Obliques say where (location:), to where (goal:), from where (source:), to whom (recipient:), with
# whom (companion:) and with what (instrument:), as in location:(house big).
# A noun with adjectives goes in parentheses, noun first: object:(wolf.PL big gray). So does a
# number, in digits, which gives the noun its number: subject:(wolf 3), and a possessor, a noun or
# a pronoun: object:(house possessor:king), companion:(dog.PL possessor:3SG.M).
# Words are English meanings with Leipzig glosses after them (PST, PFV, PL, DU, FUT, NEG on a
# verb to negate the clause, and DEF for 'the' and NDF for 'a' on a noun), and pronouns are a person and number (1SG, 3PL, 1PL.INCL, 3SG.F).
# Anything left out is present, imperfective and singular, 'we' is exclusive, and a gendered
# pronoun is masculine.
